What Usually Goes Wrong in Springfield Homes

Most water damage calls we take in Springfield trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Lines in exterior walls, garages and unheated attics are the usual trouble spots, depending on the season. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Shut off the main valve right away, because an open line can soak several rooms quickly.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. Water that backed up out of a drain or toilet bowl is not clean, so keep kids and pets out of it.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Mold can start growing within 24 to 48 hours on materials that stay wet. Many mold jobs we handle in Springfield began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.

How Drying Works on a Springfield Water Damage Job

A common question from Springfield customers is what to expect once the machines are running. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. The equipment is loud, about like several large fans, and it makes the room warm. It needs to stay on around the clock, and turning it off, shifting it or airing out the room slows everything down.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. As areas dry, we move machines to the places that are lagging and take away the ones no longer needed. You will see the numbers and hear what they mean.

Drying usually lasts 3 to 5 days. Longer jobs happen when the leak went unnoticed, when insulation or hardwood got soaked, or when more than one level is wet. When every reading matches the dry standard, Framingham Restoration removes the equipment and gives you the drying record for your Springfield property.
